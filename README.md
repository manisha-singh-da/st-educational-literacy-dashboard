## 📖 Project Overview

This project presents an interactive Power BI dashboard and policy-oriented educational literacy audit using Census of India 2001 data. The analysis examines literacy outcomes among children aged 0–14 across the three largest Scheduled Tribe communities in Dadra & Nagar Haveli:

- Varli
- Kokna
- Dhodia

The objective is to identify literacy patterns, demographic disparities, and evidence-based policy priorities through interactive data visualization.

---

## 🎯 Objectives

- Analyse literacy levels among Scheduled Tribe children aged 0–14.
- Compare literacy performance across Varli, Kokna and Dhodia communities.
- Identify age-wise literacy trends.
- Examine gender disparities in literacy.
- Study rural–urban demographic distribution.
- Develop policy recommendations based on empirical evidence.

# 🔍 Key Findings

- Varli accounts for over 72% of the tribal child population but records the lowest literacy rate (29.51%).
- Dhodia records the highest literacy rate (48.81%), although fewer than half of its children are literate.
- Literacy increases sharply after school-entry age and peaks around age 11 before declining.
- Female literacy remains consistently below male literacy across all tribes.
- Dhodia has the smallest gender gap, while Varli has the largest.
- Rural concentration alone does not fully explain literacy outcomes.

---

# 💡 Policy Insights

The analysis suggests several evidence-based policy priorities.

### Intervention Priority

Varli should receive the highest priority because it combines:

- Largest child population
- Lowest literacy rate
- Widest gender gap

---

### Educational Pipeline

The decline in literacy after age 11 suggests possible educational leakage during the transition from primary to upper-primary education.

---

### Constitutional Delivery Gap

Despite constitutional guarantees under Articles 21A, 45 and 46, substantial literacy disparities remain, indicating implementation challenges.

---

### Early Childhood Opportunity

Although literacy is expected to be zero for ages 0–6 under Census definitions, the findings reinforce the importance of strengthening Early Childhood Care and Education (ECCE).

---

### Targeted Resource Allocation

Targeting interventions toward the highest-need communities is likely to generate greater overall impact than uniform allocation.
